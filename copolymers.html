Copolymer synthesis and development

One of the core research areas of AMS Lab is the design and development of hydrophilic copolymers for the functionalization of surfaces used in biosensing applications. Through continuous innovation in polymer chemistry, we create advanced coating materials that combine ease of use, excellent antifouling properties, and versatile biofunctionalization capabilities. This research originated from the development of the copolymer poly(GMA-NAS-MAPS), initially designed as a coating material for capillary electrophoresis systems. Its primary purpose was to create stable hydrophilic coatings on the inner walls of silica capillaries, effectively suppressing electroosmotic flow and improving analytical performance. During these studies, the polymer revealed a remarkable and unexpected feature: it could rapidly and reproducibly coat a wide variety of substrates through a simple adsorption process, without requiring complex surface treatments. The combination of straightforward synthesis, fast coating procedures, strong surface adhesion, and significant resistance to nonspecific biomolecular adsorption soon attracted interest far beyond capillary electrophoresis. These characteristics made poly(GMA-NAS-MAPS) an ideal platform for biosensor development, particularly in the field of microarrays, where high probe density, low background noise, and excellent assay reproducibility are essential requirements. Another important application is the coating of nanoparticles, an important step for their functionalization with biomolecules. Over the years, the continuous evolution of this parent polymer has led to the synthesis of numerous derivatives specifically tailored to different experimental needs. By introducing alternative functional groups and molecular architectures, the properties of the coating can be finely tuned to support a broad range of immobilization strategies and bioanalytical applications. Among the most successful developments are click-compatible polymers, incorporating reactive groups such as azides, DBCO, thiols, and maleimides, enabling highly selective and efficient bioconjugation reactions. Additional modifications have introduced functionalities as exemplified by nitrilotriacetic acid (NTA) moieties for the oriented immobilization of His-tagged biomolecules. Today, polymer research remains an active and expanding field within AMS Lab. The development of new coating materials is continuously driven both by the group's internal scientific challenges and by the specific requirements emerging from collaborations with academic institutions, research centers, and industrial partners. This ongoing effort enables the creation of customized surface chemistries designed to address the evolving needs of modern biosensing, diagnostics, and analytical technologies.
